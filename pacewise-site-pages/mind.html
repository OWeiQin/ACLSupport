Mental support

When the urge to rush takes over

Wanting to get back fast is understandable. It becomes a problem when it pushes the knee past what it can recover from. These tools are for that moment.

Set a ceiling before you start

Decide the most you will do today, then stop there even if you feel great.

Write your limit, then press the button.

Ride out the urge

  • Name it: "That is the rushing urge again."
  • Wait 90 seconds before changing the plan. Most urges fade in that time.
  • Ask: would I tell a teammate to do this today?
  • Write down one thing your knee did better this week than last week.

Reframe the goal

  • Slower now means fewer setbacks, which is faster overall.
  • Every checked gate is progress you can see, even on days it feels like nothing.
  • Your target is a knee that lasts for years, not a date.
If low mood or anxiety sticks around for more than a couple of weeks, or the fear of re-injury stops you training, speak to your doctor or a counsellor. In Singapore, the SOS Samaritans line, 1767, is open 24 hours.
